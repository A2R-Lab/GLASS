Approach

Build the numerical layer once. Reuse it inside your kernels.

GLASS (GPU Linear Algebra Simple Subroutines) brings dense and structured linear algebra, spatial algebra, and geometric computation under one header-only CUDA C++ device API.

Compose inside the kernel

Call primitives directly from application GPU code, with explicit execution scopes and shared mathematical conventions. Keep intermediates on chip where the composition permits it.

Measure the placement

Offline measurements inform architecture-specific compile-time tables. A bare call keeps its block-level contract; glass::recommend<> advises scope and packing for the caller to adopt.

Validate independently

NumPy, SciPy, Pinocchio, residuals, and defining identities provide numerical references. Signed local-GPU test receipts bind outcomes to the source tree for verification in CPU-only CI.

Validation and attestation contracts →

Target robotics-scale work

GLASS targets problems small enough to be owned by one CUDA block. Larger cross-block dense problems are generally better served by conventional host-dispatched libraries.

Results

No single execution placement wins everywhere.

Measurement scope. Orin is the primary platform, with Xavier and RTX 5090 comparisons. Placement sweeps span six operations, eleven sizes, three batches, and two precisions (396 cells per GPU). Host comparisons span three operations, nine sizes, seven batches, and two precisions (378 cells), using only native GLASS candidates. Figures below retain the paper’s measured data and scales.

Execution scope matters

Across 132 Orin cells at B=8192, spanning both precisions, the fastest and slowest valid measured tiers differ by a median of 4.9× and up to 81×. The figure below shows the 66-cell fp32 slice.

Across all three measured batches, native GLASS wins 99/195 comparable fp32 cells; NVIDIA paths are selected or within the dispatch margin in the other 96.

Architecture-specific choices have a measurable cost

Orin recommendations differ from RTX 5090 in 145/396 cells and from Xavier in 162/396. Xavier’s CUDA 11.4 toolchain lacks the evaluated NVIDIA device libraries. Restricting the comparison to native candidates still changes 107/396 RTX–Orin and 66/396 Orin–Xavier recommendations.

Transferring native placement and launch configurations between architectures adds 4–20% geometric-mean runtime, depending on direction, with 95th-percentile penalties reaching 2.59×.

See the Eigen and Kokkos comparisons

The advantage is strongest at the edge

Against the per-cell best of PyTorch and JAX, native GLASS is faster in 359/378 Orin cells across fp32 and fp64, with reported geometric means of 4.3–9.5× and a peak of 73×. The corresponding RTX 5090 comparison wins 320/378 cells, with geometric means of 1.5–2.6× and a peak of 12×.

In fp32, GLASS is faster at every measured batch size for POSV through N=64, GEMM through N=48, and POTRF through N=24. The respective maxima are 113.9×, 12.1×, and 12.8×. These are measured ranges, not guarantees for every size, precision, or application. Host libraries regain advantages in parts of the larger-problem regime.

Robotics-aware composition

Fusion is a choice worth measuring.

An LQR feedback gain can be built from the same GLASS primitives as either one fused kernel or a sequence of kernels. On Orin, fusion is up to 1.75× faster at small batches; the unfused composition wins by up to 2.9× at larger batches and in resource-heavy fp64 cases.

Validated reuse

Less bespoke code. Faster robotics computation.

Sampling-based MPC

Replacing custom reductions with GLASS exposed a pre-existing normalization bug in MPPI-Generic. The repair was accepted upstream.

Against the corrected baseline, GLASS gives 1.21–1.50× speedups on Orin over 128–8192 rollouts (1.21–1.46× on RTX 5090). These timings measure the replaced computation, not a complete controller cycle.

Batched inverse kinematics

In HJCD-IK, approximately 355 lines → 29 lines of specialized numerical code become library calls—a 92% reduction.

At batch 2,000, the measured Orin runtime falls from 9.94 ms to 7.92 ms, a 1.26× speedup. The corresponding RTX 5090 result is 1.02×.

Operations span BLAS L1–L3, factorizations and solves, block-tridiagonal systems, spatial algebra, SO(3)/SE(3), quaternions, projections, and small estimation kernels. Scope and dependency availability vary by operation; the API reference documents the exact surface.

Portability here means retaining the interface while retuning across NVIDIA GPUs. On the measured Orin power modes, 30 W and 15 W increase geometric-mean runtime by 29% and 91% relative to 50 W, while each changes 9/396 recommendations. Offline tuning took 5.5 hours on Orin, 9 on Xavier, and 2 on RTX 5090 in this campaign.

Get started

Include GLASS in your CUDA code.

git clone https://github.com/A2R-Lab/GLASS.git
#include "glass.cuh"

// All threads in the block participate in one problem.
__global__ void saxpy_kernel(uint32_t n, float a, float *x, float *y) {
    glass::axpy(n, a, x, y);
}

// Launch from host code with allocated device buffers:
// saxpy_kernel<<<1, 256>>>(n, 2.0f, d_x, d_y);

Native GLASS has no MathDx dependency. NVIDIA-backed paths are optional. Start with the installation guide and complete CUDA example, then explore tuning for your GPU.

Paper

Abstract

GPU robotics lacks the reusable numerical infrastructure of mature CPU stacks, instead relying on compiler frameworks that introduce overhead or repeatedly reimplementing numerical libraries. To address this, we introduce GLASS (GPU Linear Algebra Simple Subroutines), a header-only CUDA C++ library that provides thread-, warp-, block-, and NVIDIA-backed implementations of robotics-scale linear algebra and geometric computations under one composable device API. GLASS treats implementation choice, execution scope, and launch packing as architecture-specific placement decisions determined by offline measurement and resolved statically at compile time. This is critical as the best and worst placements differ by a median of 4.9× (max 81×), with 145 of 396 recommended placements changing between a Jetson AGX Orin and an RTX 5090, and 162 of 396 versus an AGX Xavier. These stakes are highest at the edge as GLASS's advantage over the best of PyTorch and JAX is as much as 73× on the Orin versus 12× on the RTX 5090. GLASS is released open source with independent numerical oracles and source-bound local-GPU test attestation. Finally, integrating GLASS with published robotics systems both exposed a pre-existing numerical bug and improved embedded runtimes by up to 1.5×.

The placement-spread statistic covers 132 Orin cells at B=8192 across both precisions. See the result sections above for populations and comparison scope.
